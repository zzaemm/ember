How we harmonize the record

Step 1

Clean

Keep vegetation fires. Remove spots that also burn through the monsoon, such as coal fields: 4% of detections.

Step 2

Fill

Adjust days with one MODIS satellite, including Terra alone before Aqua began on 4 July 2002.

Step 3

Learn

From 2012–2019, learn VIIRS ÷ MODIS for each 27 km cell, month and day or night pass.

Step 4

Join

Use VIIRS where it flew. Use corrected MODIS before 2012 and during VIIRS outages.

k = (ΣVIIRS + λ·k_wider) ÷ (ΣMODIS + λ)

Cells with little data lean on the wider area’s ratio. λ = 50 was chosen using only the training years. A week with no detections stays at zero.

SPEAKER 2 (about 50 seconds). Four steps. First, clean: keep vegetation fires and remove spots that keep burning through the monsoon. Those turned out to sit near coal areas such as Jharia and Singrauli, and they were 4 percent of detections. Second, fill: before July 2002 only Terra was flying, so we adjust for the missing Aqua satellite. Third, learn: from 2012 to 2019, both sensors were watching, so we learn the ratio for each 27 kilometre cell, each month, and each day or night pass. Cells with little data borrow from the wider region, and this formula controls how much. Fourth, join: use real VIIRS wherever it exists, and corrected MODIS everywhere else. One thing to be clear about: this cannot recover a fire that neither sensor saw. Now [Speaker 3] will show whether it works.
