Corrected detections, 8 Oct – 18 Nov. Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, approximate area.

SPEAKER 4 (about 35 seconds). While building this we found something we were not looking for. In central India, covering Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh, detections between 8 October and 18 November rose from about 265 in 2005 to about 11,000 in 2024. Because that crosses the 2012 join, we did not trust it at first. So we checked MODIS alone, which has no sensor change at all. It rises from 39 to 1,733. So the rise is real in the satellite record. Then we checked the news. Down To Earth reported Madhya Pradesh leading India’s stubble-burning count for a second year in 2025, and a ground report from Sheopur links it to farmers switching to paddy after rain damaged other crops. Our data fits those reports, but one district’s story cannot explain the whole region, so field checks should confirm it. This is exactly what a corrected calendar is for. Now [Speaker 5] will explain how it is built.
